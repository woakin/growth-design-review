# Psychological Triggers

Leverage established cognitive biases and behavioral economics principles to reduce friction, increase perceived value, and guide user decision-making.

---

## 🪑 1. The IKEA Effect
*Definition*: People place a disproportionately high value on products they helped create, configure, or customize.
- **Application**: Involve users early through interactive onboarding quizzes, profile customization, or letting them adjust key parameters before asking for email registration or purchase commitments.

---

## ⏳ 2. The Zeigarnik Effect
*Definition*: Uncompleted or interrupted tasks create mental tension and are remembered significantly better than completed ones.
- **Application**: Use step progress bars, checklist widgets, or "Almost done" reminders that create an urge to finish the incomplete journey.

---

## 🎁 3. The Endowed Progress Effect
*Definition*: When people receive artificial or endowed progress toward a goal, they demonstrate greater effort and persistence to achieve it.
- **Application**: Start onboarding checklists with 20-25% already completed (e.g., "Account created: ✓", "First project set up: ✓") rather than starting from 0%.

---

## 👥 4. Social Proof & Bandwagon Effect
*Definition*: When uncertain, individuals look to the behavior and opinions of others to guide their own actions.
- **Application**: Showcase recent purchases ("Ordered 12 minutes ago"), verified buyer reviews, active user counts ("Join 15,000+ teams"), and recognizable partner logos near key decision points.

---

## 🎯 5. The Decoy Effect (Asymmetric Dominance)
*Definition*: Consumers tend to change preferences between two options when presented with a third option that is asymmetrically dominated.
- **Application**: In pricing tables, position a middle or higher-tier plan as overwhelmingly more attractive by including a less attractive alternative with a similar price point.

---

## 📉 6. Loss Aversion
*Definition*: The psychological pain of losing is approximately twice as intense as the joy of equivalent gain.
- **Application**: Frame propositions around what users will miss out on (e.g., "Don't lose your free trial configuration", "Save $120 today", "3 spots left at this price") rather than generic feature benefits.

---

## ⚡ 7. Authentic Scarcity & Urgency
*Definition*: Perceived scarcity of quantity or time signals high value and drives prompt decision-making.
- **Application**: Highlight real inventory counts ("Only 2 items left in stock") or time-bound delivery promises ("Order within 45 mins for next-day shipping"). Never use fake or manipulative countdown timers.

---

## 🔘 8. The Default Effect (Status Quo Bias)
*Definition*: People disproportionately stick with pre-selected options due to inertia and perceived endorsement.
- **Application**: Pre-select the recommended annual plan, optimal shipment method, or sensible default settings to reduce decision fatigue.
